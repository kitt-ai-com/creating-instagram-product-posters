# Editorial product-poster direction

Translate references into visual principles; never copy a branded composition, logo, person, or exact arrangement.

## Quality bar

A successful image feels like a campaign still, not a product cutout placed on a gradient. It combines:

- a dominant product or macro subject;
- a tactile scene with visible material character;
- directional light and purposeful shadow;
- asymmetric framing, crop tension, or a foreground aperture;
- one reserved typography zone with high contrast;
- restrained colors tied to the product.

## Choose one scene concept

### Framed encounter

Foreground objects create an aperture around the product, as if it is being discovered inside a bag, shelf, vanity, or material frame. Use realistic depth and occlusion.

### Material landscape

Place the product within a strong natural or manufactured surface: moss, stone, translucent gel, folded paper, brushed metal, fabric, or rippled glass. The material must support the product story rather than decorate it.

### Editorial macro

Use a close crop of product texture, skin, ingredient, or packaging detail with the main product integrated as a secondary focal point. Keep the hierarchy deliberate.

## Composition contract

- Product occupies roughly 45–70% of the useful frame or is cropped intentionally.
- Use one decisive angle: diagonal, top-down, low perspective, or macro crop.
- Reserve 25–35% of the canvas for copy; do not leave the whole frame empty.
- Generate no poster copy. Existing product-label text may remain.
- Avoid centered cutout + plain gradient unless the user explicitly asks for a catalog image.

## Typography contract

Typography is added after generation with the bundled compositor:

- headline: Pretendard Bold, large and compact;
- subtitle: Pretendard Regular, clearly subordinate;
- two primary text levels; optional micro-metadata only when explicitly supplied or approved;
- exact approved strings only;
- high contrast against the reserved zone.
- for stacked Korean headlines, use open leading so adjacent glyph boxes never touch; verify at full size and at 25% mobile scale.

The visual agent specifies the typography zone and recommended light or dark text color, but never names an unbundled font.
